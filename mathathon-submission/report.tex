\begin{abstract}
We seek sorting networks that are easier to construct and whose depth is easier to explain. Our lower-bound work now gives human-understandable arguments for every optimal-depth lower bound through ten wires, with no SAT or computer-found size bound as a premise. Signed-infinity restrictions yield a general two-layer transfer theorem. We use SAT-guided exploration and LLM-assisted analysis to extract reusable invariants and short certificates: a joint-sentinel invariant reduces a nine-wire cycle case to nine explicit rows, and new merger lemmas force a large interaction component four layers before the end. H9 now has hand coverage of 26 of 50 second-layer classes; its full proof remains computer-assisted. Checked structural certificates exclude 4 of 117 complete cases at thirteen wires and 3 of 609 at seventeen. At eighteen wires we have hand exclusions of selected longer prefixes, but no complete two-layer case is closed and $D(18)$ remains unresolved. A parallel construction effort supplies certified depths 26, 34, 43, and 52 at widths 128, 256, 512, and 1024. We record the unfinished AKS analysis separately. The contribution is structural understanding and concrete constructions; a complete simpler logarithmic-depth construction remains the next objective.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and current result}
Problem 47 asks for a simpler construction or explanation of logarithmic-depth sorting networks, motivated by the Ajtai--Koml\'os--Szemer\'edi theorem \cite{aks,problem}. We have pursued two connected directions: concrete constructions and AKS analysis (Sections~\ref{sec:construction}--\ref{sec:akswork}), and the finite-width lower-bound question: \emph{what structural information forces a network to spend another layer?} Small cases provide a setting in which conjectures can be falsified, certificates can be replayed, and parts of a computer-assisted argument can be turned into human proofs. Connecting these tools to a simpler asymptotic construction remains future work.

Write $D(n)$ for minimum sorting depth on $n$ wires. The published values $D(11)=8$, $D(13)=9$, and $D(17)=10$ are prior work \cite{bundala,codish}. The current construction catalogue lists $10\le D(18)\le11$ \cite{catalogue}. The numerical optima are known; our contribution begins with a self-contained chain of hand arguments through ten wires and develops structural tools for the first unresolved widths. Priority of individual elementary lemmas has not been established.

\paragraph{What this effort changed.}
Our starting point lacked a complete hand derivation of the small depth bounds. We now cover every $n\le10$ using elementary restrictions, a five-wire thin-layer obstruction, and monotonicity. In particular, the seven- and nine-wire arguments do not rely on computer-found comparator-size minima. This is completed explanatory progress even though the numerical values were already known.

The larger-width strategy is to use SAT as a source of constrained examples and certificates, and use LLM-assisted proof development to identify the invariant behind them. The objective is a statement that applies to whole families of networks. The six-cycle proof in Section~\ref{sec:h9} illustrates the gain: two moving restrictions force the next layers, and nine explicit rows finish the argument. Current exact depth results reach seventeen wires; eighteen is the first unresolved width in the cited catalogue. We are trying to move beyond the scale at which direct case search becomes the bottleneck by mining structural results from that search.

Our construction work uses sorted-pair base mergers to save layers in a multiway merging recursion. Its correctness is supported by a proof and finite compositional checks; priority relative to the full prior literature is unresolved. The AKS work remains a set of analyzed proposals, rather than an established improvement to the best published constant.

Our lower-bound contribution is a connected proof-development workflow: signed restriction arguments, finite profile lemmas, shared-suffix constraints, SAT-assisted structural exclusions, and explicit checks of their scope. Table~\ref{tab:status} gives the status at the pinned research snapshot. ``Closed'' means all continuations of that complete two-layer representative are excluded at the candidate depth. It does not mean that proportion of all networks has been searched, nor that the corresponding full theorem has been reproved by hand.

\section{Signed restrictions and a general transfer theorem}\label{sec:transfer}
A comparator $(a,b)$, $a<b$, places the smaller value on wire $a$. A layer is a matching, so no wire participates twice. A prefix is the initial sequence of layers; the remaining layers are its suffix. We number wires from zero. The zero-one principle says it suffices to sort all binary inputs: if an output inversion exists, thresholding at its larger value gives a binary inversion because comparators commute with monotone threshold maps \cite{knuth}.

Put fixed values $-\infty$ or $+\infty$ on some inputs, leaving $r$ arbitrary real inputs. A layer is \emph{blind} for that restriction if it has no real-real comparator. The type state is a vector in $\{-1,0,+1\}^n$, where $0$ denotes an arbitrary real, not the numerical value zero. For a real-position set $R$ and a matching $M$, let
\[
 e_M(R)=\#\{\{a,b\}\in M:a,b\in R\}.
\]
This counts retained comparisons. Sentinel-real comparisons have predetermined outcomes and merely route the reals. After deleting sentinel tracks, fixed renumberings can be pushed through the layers and generalized comparators untangled \cite{knuth}. Each layer retains exactly its real-real comparators. If the original network sorts, the resulting $r$-wire network sorts and has the same retained layer sizes. This pruning fact is central: it applies to \emph{every} fixed signed placement.

Only the first layer may be filled freely. Adding comparisons between idle first-layer inputs simply preprocesses an arbitrary input before a network that already sorts everything. Filling a middle layer requires a separate proof and is not assumed here.

\begin{theorem}[Two-layer transfer]\label{thm:transfer}
Let an $n$-wire sorter have $d\ge2$ layers and put $p=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. For every $0\le k\le p$, there is an $(n-k)$-wire sorter of depth at most $d$, allowing empty layers, with first two layer sizes
\[
 p-k,\qquad \max(0,|L_2|-k).
\]
In particular both sizes are at most $p-k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fill the first layer to $p$ edges. The multigraph $L_1\cup L_2$ is a disjoint union of alternating paths and even cycles, including a doubled edge when both layers use the same pair. Choose one bipartition class in each component. If there is an input idle in $L_1$, choose the class avoiding it. The union $X$ contains exactly one endpoint of every edge of both layers, lies in the support of $L_1$, and has size $p$.

Choose $k$ vertices of $X$, choosing vertices used by $L_2$ first. Put $-\infty$ on a chosen low endpoint of an $L_1$ edge and $+\infty$ on a chosen high endpoint. These sentinels stay put in $L_1$, remove exactly $k$ first-layer comparisons, and meet exactly $\min(k,|L_2|)$ distinct second-layer comparisons. Pruning and untangling give the stated counts.
\end{proof}

\status{Proved above; the implementation was also exhaustively checked on 289,420 pairs of a second-layer matching and a sentinel count for $3\le n\le11$}

Taking $k=p$ removes two blind layers and gives, for $n\ge3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
D(n)\ge D(\lceil n/2\rceil)+2.
\end{equation}
The condition $n\ge3$ matters; the formula fails for $n=2$. Two layers on three wires contain at most two comparators, with at most four swap patterns, insufficient for the six orders of distinct inputs. Thus $D(3)\ge3$, and \eqref{eq:recurrence} gives $D(5)\ge5$ and $D(9)\ge7$. Appendix~\ref{app:five} supplies the thin-layer lemma that, with $n=7,k=2$, gives $D(7)\ge6$. Pruning proves monotonicity, so these also give the even-width lower bounds through ten. Together with the trivial cases $D(1)=0$ and $D(2)=1$, this supplies hand lower bounds $0,1,3,3,5,5,6,6,7,7$ for widths one through ten. Known constructions meet them. The contribution is the complete human derivation, independent of SAT and previously computed size bounds.

\begin{corollary}[Larger finite-width consequences]\label{cor:larger}
Using the published values $D(13)=9$ and $D(17)=10$, the transfer inequality gives
\[
D(n)\ge11\ (25\le n\le32),\qquad
D(n)\ge12\ (33\le n\le48),\qquad
D(n)\ge13\ (49\le n\le64).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first interval halves to at least 13 wires, the second to at least 17, and the third to at least 25. Apply \eqref{eq:recurrence}, using the first interval in the last step.
\end{proof}
These consequences include $D(32)\ge11$, but we have not established that this is a new literature bound. They use known small-depth results; they are not independent hand proofs of those premises. Neither $D(32)=11$ nor $D(32)\ge12$ follows. The collaborator's sampled prefix exclusions do not resolve that question.

For a concrete nine-wire example, use
\[
 L_1=01\;23\;45\;67,\qquad L_2=02\;13\;46\;57,
\]
where $ab$ denotes comparator $(a,b)$ and wire 8 is idle. Put negative sentinels on 0 and 4 and positive sentinels on 3 and 7. Reals on 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 meet only sentinels in both layers. The suffix must sort five independent reals.

\section{A precise limit of counting only layer sizes}\label{sec:barrier}
Let $T(r,a)$ be the minimum depth of an $r$-wire sorter whose first two layers each have at most $a$ comparators, allowing empty layers. The transfer theorem gives $D(n)\ge T(n-k,\lfloor n/2\rfloor-k)$.

\begin{theorem}[Even-width cap barrier]\label{thm:barrier}
For $p\ge3$,
\[
\max_{p\le r\le2p-1}T(r,r-p)=D(2p-1).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $p\le r\le2p-2$, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} to an optimal sorter on $2p-1$ wires with $k'=2p-1-r$. It produces an $r$-wire sorter at depth $D(2p-1)$ with both caps at most $(p-1)-k'=r-p$. Hence every term is at most $D(2p-1)$. At $r=2p-1$ the cap is $p-1$, the maximum matching size, so the constraint is vacuous and equality holds.
\end{proof}

At width eighteen the shapes are $(r,a)=(9,0),(10,1),\ldots,(17,8)$. Each is realizable in depth at most ten because $D(17)=10$ is known. Improving the small-sorter bounds for \emph{these two caps alone} cannot prove eleven. This is a theorem about this specific relaxation, not a limitation of every sentinel argument. It motivates keeping the positions, signs, and joint evolution of the restrictions.

\section{Families that share a suffix}\label{sec:families}
For a two-layer prefix $P=(L_1,L_2)$ with maximal $L_1$, let $\F(P)$ contain the distinct signed type states after $L_2$ obtained by placing exactly $p=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ sentinels so that both layers are blind. There are $r=\lceil n/2\rceil$ reals. Every first-layer edge must have exactly one sentinel. After $L_1$, its type is either $(-1,0)$ or $(0,+1)$ in low/high order; the possible idle wire is real. Thus $2^p$ normalized choices cover all initial placements.

Call a path on $2m$ vertices whose endpoints are idle in $L_2$ a \emph{stick}. The possible odd path through the wire idle in $L_1$ is a \emph{head}. Label each vertex by its low or high role in $L_1$. A cycle is \emph{frozen} if every $L_2$ edge joins equal roles, and flexible otherwise.

\begin{theorem}[Exact restriction count]\label{thm:family}
If there are $c$ flexible cycles and sticks of sizes $2m_1,\ldots,2m_s$, then
\[
 |\F(P)|=2^c\prod_{i=1}^s(m_i+1).
\]
Distinct states in $\F(P)$ have distinct real-position sets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Before $L_2$, the real vertices form an independent set in $L_1\cup L_2$, choosing one vertex of each first-layer pair and the idle vertex if present. On a head, alternation from the idle real vertex forces one choice. Traverse a stick beginning with an $L_1$ edge and encode left/right choices in successive pairs by 0/1. Independence forbids the adjacent pattern 10, leaving exactly $0^j1^{m-j}$ for $0\le j\le m$. An interior transition identifies a unique second-layer edge with two sentinels, while the two endpoint choices differ in which idle endpoint is real. The resulting real sets are all different.

On a cycle, only the two alternating color classes remain. On a same-role second-layer edge the two choices supply the same sentinel sign and the same real output position. On an opposite-role edge the sign changes and the real output switches endpoints. Thus the two states coincide exactly for a frozen cycle. Components are disjoint, so their contributions multiply and the real sets identify the states.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:modfour}
Every such prefix on $n=4q+2$ wires has at least two states in $\F(P)$, and this is sharp.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A stick already contributes at least two. Without sticks all components are cycles. A frozen cycle with $m$ first-layer edges has $m/2$ low-low and $m/2$ high-high second-layer edges, so its size is divisible by four. Frozen cycles cannot cover $4q+2$ vertices. Sharpness follows from $q$ frozen four-cycles and an isolated first-layer pair.
\end{proof}

Seventeen wires can have only one state: four frozen four-cycles and an idle wire. Eighteen necessarily has at least two. This distinguishes the restriction families without yet distinguishing their optimal depths. Among the 411 eighteen-wire saturated representatives, 51 have exactly two states. Their real-set intersections and counts are $8:12$, $6:18$, $4:15$, $2:5$, and $0:1$. These are finite enumeration results, not claims that more restrictions force an extra layer.

\begin{lemma}[Transport coupling]\label{lem:coupling}
Comparator layers are nonexpansive in $\ell_1$ on signed type vectors. If states $x,y$ have the same number of reals and $\|x-y\|_1\le2h$, then after any common suffix prefix their real sets differ by at most $h$ exchanges. For the next matching $M$, their retained comparator counts differ by at most $h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any four ordered values,
\[
|\min(a,b)-\min(c,d)|+|\max(a,b)-\max(c,d)|
\le |a-c|+|b-d|.
\]
This follows by uncrossing the two pairs on the line. Sum it over disjoint comparators, including unchanged idle coordinates. Every position real in only one state contributes at least one to the distance. Equal real counts make the symmetric difference twice the number of exchanges. An edge counted only for one real set uses one of its exclusive vertices, and a matching uses each vertex at most once. The difference of edge counts is therefore at most the number of exchanges.
\end{proof}

Neighboring stick choices differ by replacing $(-1,0)$ with $(0,+1)$ on one first-layer pair. Their initial distance is two. Consequently their corresponding retained layer counts differ by at most one throughout every common suffix. Their real sets may coincide and later separate. The invariant is the signed-distance bound, not permanent merging. Local contraction was checked on all 81 ternary quadruples; the recorded transport audit checks 526 stick components and 167,800 sampled transport steps. The universal claim follows from the proof, not the sampling.

\section{Why another entirely blind layer is insufficient}\label{sec:blindbarrier}
We use the saturated two-layer cover of Bundala et al. \cite{bundala}. Its completeness and normal forms are imported results. In this normal form repeated-edge cycles are absent, nontrivial sticks have at least six vertices, and an isolated first-layer pair can coexist only with cycles.

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction to universal third-layer blinding]\label{thm:third}
For every saturated two-layer prefix on even $n=2p\ge18$, some perfect third matching defeats every placement of $p$ signed sentinels that blinds the first two layers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First $|\F(P)|\le2^{2p/3}$. Without an isolated first-layer pair, a stick using $m\ge3$ first-layer edges contributes $m+1\le4^{m/3}$, and a cycle using $m\ge2$ contributes at most $2\le4^{m/3}$. Multiply over components. With an isolated pair, all other components are cycles, giving at most $2^{(p+1)/2}\le2^{2p/3}$ for $p\ge3$.

A uniformly random perfect matching on $2p$ wires leaves a fixed $p$-element real set blind precisely when every edge crosses to its complement. This happens for $p!$ of the $(2p-1)!!$ matchings, with probability $2^p/\binom{2p}{p}$. The union bound gives
\[
\Pr(\text{some state remains blind})\le
\frac{2^{5p/3}}{\binom{2p}{p}}.
\]
At $p=9$ this is $32768/48620<1$. The ratio of consecutive upper bounds is $2^{5/3}(p+1)/(2(2p+1))<1$ for $p\ge9$. Hence a matching defeating every state exists for all these widths. Normalization covers all initial signed placements.
\end{proof}

At eighteen wires there are $17!!=34,459,425$ perfect matchings and at most 64 blind states, so at least $34,459,425-64\cdot9!=11,235,105$ third layers defeat them all. The saved certificate supplies one witness for each of the 411 representatives. A fresh replay checked all 411 witnesses and 5,078 normalized placements. These witnesses are obstructions to a proof tactic; they are not sorting networks and do not refute depth-ten completion. Allowing a few real comparisons and rescheduling them is a different route.

\subsubsection*{From a search class to a nine-row human proof}
Consider $L_1=01\;23\;45$ and $L_2=03\;14\;25$, with wires 6, 7, 8 initially idle. The key idea is to follow two restrictions through the \emph{same} later matchings. In one run put only negative sentinels; in the other put only positive sentinels. If their supports are disjoint, the joint wire types are
\[
(-\infty,\text{real})< (\text{real},\text{real}) <(\text{real},+\infty).
\]
A comparator simply sorts these joint types, so disjointness is invariant. With three sentinels in each run on nine wires, their six-real sets always intersect in three wires. A matching cannot pair both sets internally: it would pair their odd-sized intersection internally.

Use negative sentinels on $0,2,4$ in run $P$ and positive sentinels on $1,3,5$ in run $Q$. Both initial layers are blind. After $L_2$, put $A=\{0,1,2\}$, $B=\{3,4,5\}$, $C=\{6,7,8\}$: the real sets are $B\cup C$ and $A\cup C$. A five-layer six-wire sorter needs at least two gates in each of its first two layers and three in at least one. The first assertion follows by one-sentinel reduction to $D(5)\ge5$; if both layers had two gates, a sentinel on their common support would give the forbidden five-wire thin profile of Appendix~\ref{app:five}. Thus the two restrictions force retained counts $(3,2)$ and $(2,3)$ in $L_3,L_4$.

After a duality and relabelling of the idle wires, this forces
\[
L_3=(A\setminus\{a\})(B\setminus\{b\})(b,6)(7,8),\quad
L_4=(a,x)(x',y')(b,y)(6,z),
\]
where $a\in A$, $b\in B$, $\{x,x'\}=A\setminus\{a\}$, $\{y,y'\}=\{7,8\}$ and $z\in B\setminus\{b\}$. A two-element set in parentheses denotes its comparison. Indeed, the $P$-perfect third matching has an odd number of $B$--$C$ edges; three would leave too few $Q$ comparisons, so it has one. The fourth matching must pair $Q$'s updated six-real set while giving two $P$ comparisons. Removing consecutive repeated gates gives the displayed form. The full orientation and symmetry argument is in \file{depth-hand-proofs/h9-cycle-case.md}.

The resulting 72 forms are covered by nine symbolic rows. Here $+i,-i$ fix the original input to the indicated infinity; unspecified parameters are unrestricted.

\subsection{Common rank-position constraints}
After $t$ layers let $P_t(k)$ be the possible wires holding rank $k$, over permutations of distinct ranks $0,\ldots,n-1$, and $Q_t(c)$ the possible ranks on wire $c$. If $s$ layers remain, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cones}
|P_t(k)|\le2^s,\qquad |Q_t(c)|\le2^s.
\end{equation}
These conditions have a short proof: the backward cone of output $k$ and the forward cone of wire $c$ at most double at each matching layer. The same suffix must realize all cones simultaneously. If two layers $M,N$ remain and $B_M(S)$ adds the partners in $M$ of vertices of $S$, then
\[
P_t(k)\subseteq B_M(B_N(\{k\}))\quad\text{for every }k,
\]
with the same $M,N$ for all ranks.

Adjacent binary-input probes compute the relation exactly. Compare runs on $x$ and $x+e_i$, where $x_i=0$ and $x$ has $n-1-k$ ones. Monotonicity and preservation of the number of ones imply that the runs differ at exactly one wire throughout. Assign rank $k$ to input $i$, larger ranks to the ones, and smaller ranks to the other zeros. The differing wire is the position of rank $k$. Conversely every permutation induces such a threshold pair. The encoders simulate a common prefix, rather than choosing arbitrary supports independently.

If $a$ is the fewest input zeros that can make this wire output zero and $b$ the fewest input ones that can make it output one, thresholding gives endpoints $a-1$ and $n-b$. \subsection{A component obstruction at arbitrary width}\label{sec:components}
The structural follow-up proves that merging independently sorted blocks of lengths $(4,3,2)$, $(3,3,3)$, or $(4,4,1)$ needs at least five layers. These are hand arguments using sentinel restrictions, small profiles, and dependency cones; their supplementary proofs use no SAT theorem as a premise. They give the following reusable conclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Nine-real component requirement]
Four layers before the end of any sorting network, every signed restriction leaving nine real inputs has an interaction component containing at least five real tracks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Join two logical real tracks when they have been compared before the cut. Sentinel comparisons only route the track labels. If every component had size at most four, its partition of nine could be packed into $(4,3,2)$, $(3,3,3)$, or $(4,4,1)$: two size-four parts give the last pattern; one size-four part leaves a partition of five into sizes at most three, which splits into three and two; three triples give the second pattern; all other combinations of triples, pairs, and singletons fit the first.

Relabel and untangle the pruned network with those three packed blocks consecutive. The prefix has no comparison between blocks, so it fixes every input sorted separately within each block. Its image therefore contains the full corresponding merger family, regardless of whether the prefix sorts its own components. Four remaining layers would merge that family, contradicting the hand merger bounds.
\end{proof}
This detects restrictions with many retained comparisons; it does not require earlier layers to be blind. It is the kind of structural result we seek to extract from SAT-guided experiments. The recorded component screen rejects 34 of 117 saved thirteen-wire \emph{four-layer witnesses}, while a new case-116 witness survives. Those rejections do not close complete two-layer cases. All six sampled eighteen-wire six-layer prefixes pass the screen. Fresh independent controls checked 108 small prefix/mode combinations, 8,676 signed assignments, all 30 partitions of nine, and 68,608 binary trace replays. A known depth-eleven eighteen-wire sorter was checked on all 262,144 binary inputs and passes the component condition. The whole-case census remains 4/117, 3/609, and 0/411.

\section{A concrete eighteen-wire exclusion}\label{sec:example}
The following example shows what a short extracted certificate looks like. It excludes all depth-ten completions of one \emph{specified five-layer prefix}, rather than a complete two-layer representative. Appendix~\ref{app:prefix} lists every comparator and sentinel placement.

For each of four placements, seven arbitrary reals survive. Track them through the five layers, deleting sentinel comparisons. All retained comparisons use two disjoint pairs of real input tracks. For any pair, its last comparison determines the final orientation; earlier comparisons of that pair are redundant after fixed routing is accounted for. Comparisons on disjoint pairs commute. Thus the five-layer restriction compresses to one layer of two comparisons.

We use the early-profile fact H7: a depth-six seven-wire sorter cannot have at most two comparators in both of its first two layers. Its checkpoint hand proof uses the one-thin-layer lemmas P7 and Q7 and a finite probe table. A fresh replay checked its 26 probes, seven third-layer representatives, and 22 final checks. Appendix~\ref{app:h7} explains the proof dependencies and the scope of the finite Q7 data. This dependency is more substantial than the pricing inequality itself.

If the five-layer prefix had a depth-ten completion, compression would give each seven-real restriction a depth-six sorter whose first layer has two gates. H7 therefore requires three real-real gates in the next original layer, $L_6$, for every restriction. At the cut after $L_5$, the real sets have form
\[
C\cup\{a,b,x\},\quad C\cup\{b,c,y\},\quad
C\cup\{a,b,c\},\quad C\cup\{a,c,z\},
\]
where
\[
C=\{7,10,12,13\},\quad(a,b,c)=(9,11,16),\quad(x,y,z)=(8,4,14).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Four-set price certificate]\label{lem:price}
For these sets and every matching $M$, $\sum_{i=1}^4e_M(R_i)\le11$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Give each vertex of $C$ price 2, each of $a,b,c$ price 1, and every other vertex price 0. The multiplicity of an edge across the four real sets is at most the sum of its endpoint prices: it is 4 for a common/common edge, 3 for a common/fork edge, 2 for a pair among $a,b,c$, and at most 1 for all other occurring edges. Matching edges are disjoint, so the sum of multiplicities is at most the total price $2|C|+3=11$.
\end{proof}

The required four peaks would instead give $\sum_i e_{L_6}(R_i)=12$. This contradiction proves the claimed prefix exclusion, given H7 and the explicit track identities. The direct verifier checks all $4\cdot7!=20,160$ distinct-real assignments and all $\binom{18}{2}=153$ edge-price inequalities. These checks were rerun for this report. Discovery used a search over signed placements, but checking the four explicit restrictions and proving the inequality do not require that search. Two additional six-layer prefix exclusions are recorded in the checkpoint using related compression and pricing arguments. None closes a whole member of the 411-case two-layer cover.

Let $M(k,m)$ be minimum depth to merge $k$ sorted lists of length $m$. A sixteen-wire sorter whose first layer sorts eight adjacent pairs leaves, after removing that layer, an eight-layer merger of eight sorted pairs. Conversely, one pair layer followed by such a merger sorts sixteen inputs, so the known $D(16)=9$ gives $M(8,2)=8$.

\begin{theorem}[Multiway odd-even step]\label{thm:merge}
For positive integers $k,m$, $M(k,2m)\le M(k,m)+\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Merge the even-indexed and odd-indexed elements of the input lists separately and in parallel. Interleave the two sorted outputs. If list $i$ has $z_i$ zeros, its even and odd parts have $\lceil z_i/2\rceil$ and $\lfloor z_i/2\rfloor$ zeros. Thus the interleaving has the form $0^{2b}(01)^\delta1^*$ with $0\le\delta\le k$.

Put $t=\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$. For $s=2^{t-1},2^{t-2},\ldots,1$, compare positions $2i+1$ and $2i+2s$ whenever both exist. Each layer is a matching. If $\delta\le s$ no inversion is swapped. If $s<\delta\le2s$, the swapped pairs have offsets $2j+1,2j+2s$ from the start of the alternating block, for $0\le j<\delta-s$. The resulting block is $0^{2(\delta-s)}(01)^{2s-\delta}$ followed by ones. Hence its new alternating length is at most $s$. Induction over the cleanup layers leaves $\delta\le1$, which is sorted. The zero-one principle proves correctness on arbitrary ordered data. For $k=1$ the merge is already sorted and the statement is immediate.
\end{proof}

For power-of-two $m\ge2$, this gives $M(8,m)\le8+3\log_2(m/2)$. Therefore $M(8,16)\le17$ and $M(8,128)\le26$, yielding depths $9+17=26$ and $26+26=52$. Standard binary odd-even merges have depths eight and nine at widths 256 and 512, giving the middle two rows. The earlier collaborator report compares the 128/1024 constructions with arithmetic from Lee--Batcher's formula, finding a one-layer saving in that comparison. That comparison is not a complete prior-art search.

\section{Five-wire thin layers and the seven-wire consequence}\label{app:five}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:five}
No five-wire sorter of at most five layers has at most one comparator in each of its first two layers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a layer is empty, or the two gates are equal or disjoint, delete a repetition or commute the gates into one layer. A depth-four sorter would result, contrary to $D(5)\ge5$. Otherwise the gates are $\{p,q\}$ and $\{q,s\}$, leaving $y,z$ idle. Put on $q$ the infinity $A$ that stays there in the first gate. After the second gate it is at $q'$; let $s'$ be the other output of that gate. Two sentinels can blind both layers and leave each of the following real triples:
\[
\{p,s',z\},\quad\{p,s',y\},\quad\{p,y,z\},\quad
\{q,y,z\},\quad\{s,y,z\}.
\]
The first three use $A$ on $q$ and a sentinel on $y,z,s$, respectively. The last two use two equal positive or two equal negative sentinels on $p,q$.

The third layer must hit every triple with an internal edge; otherwise three reals would have only two active layers. To hit $\{p,y,z\},\{q,y,z\},\{s,y,z\}$ without $yz$ needs three edges incident to $y$ or $z$, impossible in a matching. Thus $yz$ is a gate. The first two triples then force $ps'$. The three gates on $p,q,s$ sort that triple: $q'$ already holds its extreme value and $ps'$ sorts the other two. The pair $y,z$ is also sorted. Every sorted triple/pair input occurs because it is fixed by these gates.

Two remaining layers would merge a sorted triple $(a_0,a_1,a_2)$ and pair $(b_0,b_1)$. The median depends on each of the five positions, witnessed for $1<x<8$ by
\[
(x,8,9\mid0,1),\ (0,x,9\mid1,8),\ (0,1,x\mid8,9),\
(0,1,9\mid x,8),\ (0,8,9\mid1,x).
\]
But an output after two layers depends on at most four inputs. Contradiction.
\end{proof}
The transfer theorem for $n=7,k=2$ would turn a five-layer seven-wire sorter into exactly this forbidden five-wire shape. Hence $D(7)\ge6$.

\section{Claim ledger and audit protocol}\label{app:audit}
This appendix prioritizes auditability. Stable identifiers C01--C20 identify conclusions; R01--R14 identify fresh finite replays; L01--L02 identify fresh Lean checks; H01 identifies file-integrity checks. The machine-readable companion is \file{supplementary/claim-ledger.json}. Each entry binds its evidence paths to full SHA-256 digests and byte counts. Dependency edges below mean mathematical premises, not merely experiments that suggested the result.

\paragraph{Evidence semantics.}
A hand proof is a mathematical argument whose completeness must be assessed from its stated reductions and tables. A finite replay checks only its declared domain. A Lean-verified claim means the stated declaration was accepted with the reported transitive axioms, after checking that its definitions express the intended statement. An historical DRAT certificate concerns a particular CNF; correctness of the encoding and the cover reduction remain separate obligations. A hash binds bytes and does not establish mathematical truth. A future-work or unfinished-proposal entry is not an established theorem.

\paragraph{Scope of completeness.}
All small lower bounds through ten have hand arguments. Full H9 has computational evidence but only 26/50 hand classes. The whole-case census at widths 13, 17 and 18 is 4/117, 3/609 and 0/411. Neither a selected longer-prefix exclusion, rejection of a saved relaxation witness, nor a proposed AKS parameter calculation is promoted to a complete theorem beyond that scope.

\subsection{Formal statement and dependency audit}
The exact public statements, written with ASCII Lean aliases for legibility, are:
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
theorem remove_two_layers {n : Nat}
    (A B : Layer (Fin n)) (N : List (Layer (Fin n)))
    (hs : Sorts (A :: B :: N)) :
    Exists (fun M : List (Layer (Fin ((n + 1) / 2))) =>
      And (Sorts M) (M.length = N.length))

theorem D_ceil_half_add_two (n : Nat) (hn : 3 <= n) :
    D ((n + 1) / 2) + 2 <= D n
\end{Verbatim}
The source uses the equivalent usual mathematical Unicode notation. The input quantifier is over every sorting network, with no hidden first-layer normal-form assumption. \file{exists_full_first} proves the necessary first-layer filling step. \file{two_colour} constructs the alternating live/dead selection; pruning carries live labels through fixed sentinel routing. \file{reduced_sorts} preserves sorting, \file{reduced_length} preserves the tail length, and \file{card_live} proves the surviving width. For the depth inequality, \file{sorter_exists} makes the minimization defining $D$ well-defined, \file{two_le_length} supplies the two initial layers when $n\ge3$, and \file{D_le_length} compares the reduced witness with the minimum.

The semantic audit checks: \file{Layer} is an involution, so its two-cycles are disjoint comparators; \file{Layer.run} sends minima to smaller wire indices; list execution runs layers in order; \file{Sorts} means all Boolean inputs have monotone output; and \file{Sorts.mono_run} proves the extension to every linearly ordered value type. The integer expression \texttt{(n + 1) / 2} means $\lceil n/2\rceil$. The project does not assume the desired depth inequality as an axiom.

\section{Expanded structural proof details}\label{app:expanded}
These details make the short arguments auditable independently of the discovery search. The full $(4,3,2)$ contact tables remain in the exact supplementary notes bound by C15; the two other merger proofs and the cycle classification are expanded here.

\subsection{The six-wire profile used by the cycle and merger arguments}
A six-wire sorter of at most five layers needs at least two comparators in each of its first two layers, and at least one of those layers has three. If the first layer has at most one gate, a sentinel blinds it and leaves five reals with at most four active layers, contradicting $D(5)\ge5$. If the second layer has one gate, choose an endpoint and the sentinel sign that holds that endpoint fixed through the first layer. It blinds the second layer and gives the same contradiction; an empty layer is immediate. If both layers have two gates, their four-element supports intersect. A sentinel fixed at a common vertex in the first layer removes one gate in each. The remaining five-wire sorter has two thin first layers, contradicting Appendix A. Thus the allowed initial size pairs are $(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)$. This argument uses neither H7 nor full H9.

\subsection{Why the cycle has exactly the displayed 72 forms}
Use $A,B,C,P,Q$ from Section~\ref{sec:h9}. The two restrictions have complementary peak patterns $(3,2)$ and $(2,3)$ in layers three and four. Negation followed by $i\mapsto5-i$ on the first six wires and $6\leftrightarrow8$ on the last three exchanges the roles of $P,Q$ while preserving the first two comparator layers, including orientation. Untangling later reversed comparators preserves sorting and real-real counts. Hence assume $P$ has three gates in $L_3$. Delete any consecutive repeated gate; its second copy cannot fire, and this deletion preserves the specified first two layers.

Since $L_3$ pairs $B\cup C$, the number $k$ of $B$--$C$ edges is odd. Its comparisons among $Q$'s reals lie inside $A$ or inside $C$, at most $1+(3-k)/2$. This must be at least two, so $k=1$. A permutation of the three initially idle wires makes the cross edge end at 6, with the remaining pair $78$. Thus for $a\in A,b\in B$,
\[
L_3=(A\setminus\{a\})(B\setminus\{b\})(b,6)(7,8).
\]
This also forces the internal $A$ edge. Afterward $P$'s real set is still $B\cup C$, whereas $Q$'s is $A\cup\{b,7,8\}$. The fourth matching pairs the latter six vertices. To retain two $P$ comparisons it needs one edge inside $\{b,7,8\}$ and one inside $\{6\}\cup(B\setminus\{b\})$. Excluding consecutive repetitions makes these $(b,y)$ and $(6,z)$, where $y\in\{7,8\}$ and $z\in B\setminus\{b\}$. Pairing the remaining $Q$ vertices then gives
\[
L_4=(a,x)(x',y')(b,y)(6,z),\qquad
\{x,x'\}=A\setminus\{a\},\quad\{y,y'\}=\{7,8\}.
\]
There are $3\cdot3\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=72$ parameter instances. This is a classification proof, not an inference from the number found by an enumerator.

For an explicit audit of the nine-row table, the six distinct sentinel placements have the following type vectors after $L_2$. Here type 0 means an arbitrary real, not a numerical assignment of equal inputs.

Apply the displayed $L_3,L_4$ to these types and count zero-zero edges. For row 1, the real set after $L_2$ is $\{0,5,6,7,8\}$, so only $78$ retains a comparison in $L_3$. It becomes $\{0,3,4,7,8\}$; in $L_4$ only $(b,y)$ retains a comparison. The other table rows use the same calculation. The exceptional choices of $(x,y)$ are explicitly separated in the nine rows, and $z$ is unrestricted throughout. Each row gives the five-wire thin contradiction.

\paragraph{Limit at even width.}
Disjoint negative-only and positive-only sentinel supports preserve real-set intersection exactly. On even ambient width, two even real-set sizes give an even intersection, so this particular odd-intersection contradiction does not directly apply at width 18. If the supports overlap, add the joint type $d=(-\infty,+\infty)$ to $a=(-\infty,0)$, $c=(0,0)$ and $b=(0,+\infty)$. Only a comparison of $c,d$ changes joint-type multiplicities, producing $a,b$. If a layer contains $h$ such comparisons, both support intersection and real-set intersection drop by $h$, while $2m_P\le r_P-h$ and $2m_Q\le r_Q-h$ for retained gate counts $m_P,m_Q$. This identifies an event that a larger proof would need to control; it is not a completed eighteen-wire obstruction.

\subsection{Three sorted triples need five merger layers}
Assume four layers merge three independently sorted triples; let $N$ be the first matching. Remove its internal-triple gates, which never fire. Keep either adjacent pair from each triple and replace its omitted endpoint by the appropriate infinity. The eight choices leave three sorted pairs. Prefixing one pair-sorting layer creates a six-wire sorter of depth five. The six-wire profile above forces at least two real-real edges of $N$ in every restriction, for a total demand of 16.

Call the central position of each triple a middle. A middle-middle edge belongs to all eight real sets, a middle-endpoint edge to four, and an endpoint-endpoint edge to two. If $N$ has no middle-middle edge, three middles and at most four gates give weight at most $3\cdot4+2=14$. With a middle-middle edge, the remaining middle permits at most one middle-endpoint edge; the total is at most $8+4+2+2=16$. Equality is therefore necessary: $N$ has exactly these four edge types, leaves one endpoint $v$ idle, and every restriction retains exactly two gates.

Let $u$ be the other endpoint of the triple containing $v$. Consider the four deletion triples consisting of $u$ and one endpoint from each of the other two input triples. All deleted endpoints are used by $N$. Exactly two of $N$'s four gates remain real-real, so the three deleted endpoints occupy exactly two gates. Every deletion triple must therefore contain an endpoint-endpoint edge of $N$.

There are only two such edges. At most one uses $u$, and it occurs in at most two of the four deletion triples. Any other uses neither endpoint of $u$'s triple, since $v$ is idle, and occurs in at most one deletion triple. The two edges cover at most three of four triples. Contradiction. The eight restrictions and their edge weights constitute the proof; the 1,126-matching replay is corroboration.

\subsection{Two blocks of four and one singleton need five layers}
First a sorted triple and two independent singletons require at least four merger layers. In an alleged three-layer merger, delete internal-triple gates from its first layer. Both singletons must be used there: an idle singleton would need to reach all five possible ranks in two layers, whereas a forward cone has at most four positions. If the singletons compare with each other, the remaining two layers must merge a sorted triple and sorted pair. Their median can start at each of five positions, exceeding its backward cone of size four. Otherwise each singleton compares with a distinct triple position. Set those two positions to negative infinities below the unpaired position and positive infinities above it, preserving the triple's order. The first layer is blind on the two singletons and the unpaired real. Two layers would sort three arbitrary reals, contradicting $D(3)\ge3$.

Now assume four layers merge sorted blocks $A=(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)$, $B=(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)$, and singleton $c$. Remove first-layer comparisons internal to a sorted block. The singleton must be used in the first matching $N$, since otherwise its nine possible ranks would have only a three-layer forward cone of size eight. Exchange the names of the two blocks so its partner is $a_j$.

For each of the three other positions $a_i$, keep $a_i,c$ and either $B$ triple $b_0b_1b_2$ or $b_1b_2b_3$ real, filling all omitted positions with signs that preserve their block order. If $N$ were blind on this set, the last three layers would merge a triple and two singletons, which was just excluded. The edge at $c$ is blind because $a_j$ is a sentinel. There are no internal $B$ gates. Thus $a_i$ must be paired into the selected triple of $B$. Its same partner must serve both choices, so it belongs to $\{b_1,b_2\}$. Three different $a_i$ require three distinct partners in a two-element set, impossible.

These arguments permit nonperfect matchings and fixed embeddings of the blocks, after relabelling and untangling. They do not fill an arbitrary middle layer. Together with the separately bound $(4,3,2)$ contact proof, they discharge the merger premises of C16.
